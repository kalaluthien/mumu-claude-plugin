# Decision Points

Choose what to ask the owner and what to settle yourself, so each question earns its reading time.

## Domain

- **Decision point**: a place in the plan where more than one option is reasonable and the choice changes the work.
- **Action differs**: the owner's answer would make you do something else; if every answer leads to the same next step, there is nothing to ask.
- **Reversal cost**: what it takes to undo the choice after it lands: data migrated, messages sent, an interface published, people told.
- **Expected information**: how much you would learn from the answer compared with what you already hold in the code, the request and the records.
- **Entropy**: how evenly your belief spreads over the options. Read it as judgement: one option clearly ahead is low, several close together are high.

## Steps

1. List each choice the task holds, including the ones you made without noticing.
2. For each, write what you would do under each option. Where the action differs nowhere, drop the choice or keep it as a `note`.
3. Look for the answer yourself first: read the code, the request and past decisions. A fact you can find is not a Decision.
4. Weigh what is left by reversal cost: a choice that is cheap to undo stays with you, written as a Part, with its reason.
5. Weigh the rest by expected information: ask where you are torn and the owner knows something you do not; where entropy is low, decide and let the owner read your pick.
6. Keep the Decisions few. Past 5, check that each still earns its place, or can wait until an earlier answer is in.
7. Order them so a later one depends on an earlier one, with `requires`, and never ask what an earlier answer settles.
8. For each Decision, make the `pick` the option you would take with no answer, and put in its Parts the consequence that separates it from the others.

## Rules

- **Ask where it pays**: a Decision is worth asking when the action differs, the reversal cost is high, and the expected information is high; two out of three is a note or your own call.
- **Judge, do not compute**: no computed probabilities and no scores; entropy here is a way to think, since numbers made up for one plan only look precise.
- **Silence is not consent**: a Decision the owner left open stays open; a choice with a high reversal cost waits for its answer.
- **Name the stake**: write the question as the thing to be decided, not as a topic, so the owner sees what changes.
- **Facts over rationale**: show the consequences of each option; a long reason in favour of your pick is pressure, not information.
- **One question each**: split a Decision that holds two choices; its options would multiply and neither answer would be clear.
- **Say the assumption**: a choice you settled with a real but low reversal cost goes on the page as a Part, so the owner can strike it.
