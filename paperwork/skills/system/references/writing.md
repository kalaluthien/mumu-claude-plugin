# writing

Word every text of a contract so that a reader new to the subject's field takes each level's point from its first line, meets one word for one concept, and reads nothing the rebuild does not need.

## Domain

- **name**: a title, a label, a term or an edge's label. It is English and shows as written. A level's, a scenario's and a claim's title is a slug, lowercase words and digits joined by `-`, such as `inside-server`.
- **description**: every other text: a `purpose`, an `about`, a `meaning`, an item's text, a step's `note`, a `statement`, a question's `asks`. It is English, or Korean when the reader asks for Korean, one language through the contract.
- **lead**: the description a reader meets before what sits under it. The contract's `purpose` leads the levels, a level's `about` its areas, an area's `purpose` item, or its `why` where its tag has no `purpose`, its nodes, a node's `purpose` its items, a scenario's `purpose` its steps.
- **reader**: the person who rebuilds the subject from the contract alone. The reader knows software in general, never the subject's field, its sources or the history of the contract.
- **structure**: the levels, areas, nodes, edges and scenarios with their names, before any description.
- **other word**: a second word for the concept a term names. A term lists them in `avoid`, and the checker refuses each in every text.
- **alias**: a word a reader may type to find a record that its name and texts lack. A node, an area or a term lists them in `aliases`, which no text shows, so an alias may be Korean.
- **settled word**: a Korean word that 표준국어대사전 (`https://stdict.korean.go.kr`) holds in the sense meant, or that the subject's own Korean documents use more than its transliteration.
- **meta narration**: a text about the contract instead of the subject: why a text is worded so, what changed from an earlier version, how to read the page, or what the owner or a requester asked for.
- **revision pass**: one read of every description by a subagent with a fresh context, briefed as the reader, that lists each break of the rules below with a rewrite.

## Steps

1. List the terms: each word of the subject's business, technique, theory, profession or guideline that a reader must take in one sense becomes a `term` with its `meaning`, and its `avoid` lists the other words the sources, a translation or habit would say for it; in a Korean contract that includes the literal Korean coinage a writer reaches for, such as `출입구` for `endpoint` or `증표` for `token`, since an empty `avoid` lets the checker pass any of them.
2. Write the structure first: every level, area, node, edge and scenario with its name and no description yet, such as the levels `whole` and `inside-server` with their nodes and edges by label. Read the outline once and move or rename until each level reads as one picture; a description written before the structure settles is rewritten when it moves.
3. Write the leads from the top down.
4. List the aliases: for each record a reader would look for by another word, add its Korean name, its abbreviation or the expansion of one, and a common other name, such as `아마존` and `Amazon Web Services` for `AWS`; a word of a term's `avoid` may be its alias, since a reader types the word they know.
5. Write what sits under each lead: the items, the notes, the statements.
6. Refine in rounds: `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/system/scripts/check-contract.py outline <contract> [level]` prints each lead with its parts. The first line must raise the question each level's lead answers; hold each level against the rules under Structure, rewrite what breaks one, and print the outline again, until a round changes nothing.
7. Sweep the words of each Korean description against **Settled word first**: list every technical noun and phrase, mark each settled Sino-Korean word, transliteration or English word, and replace each one that is neither, a literal translation or a word coined for the occasion, such as `해독` for decoding or `걸리는 시간이 늘 같은` for constant-time; add the replaced word to the term's `avoid` when a term names that concept.
8. Run the revision pass: start a subagent with a fresh context and this brief: "You know software but not this subject's field. Read every description of `<contract>`. List each sentence that holds two ideas or more, chains clauses by `and`, `which`, `so that`, `-며`, `-고` or `-는데`, uses a field word or an abbreviation no term or nearby clause explains, or breaks **No meta narration**, **No filler** or **Hedge only the unknown** in `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/system/references/writing.md`; give each its rewrite as several plain sentences." Apply each rewrite that keeps every fact, then run the pass again on the changed descriptions until it lists none. The writer never runs the pass alone, since the writer already knows what each sentence means.
9. Check: the `check` call refuses what its `rules` call lists; every other rule here is the writer's to keep.

## Rules

### Structure

- **Lead says the point**: a lead states what is true of all under it, in words a reader could deny, such as "A courier and a receiver never meet; one code joins them". It never only announces what follows, such as "The parts of the locker" or "Who takes part and what passes between them".
- **Lead covers its parts**: a lead is true of every part under it and promises nothing that no part delivers. A part the lead does not cover moves to the level whose lead does, or the lead is rewritten.
- **Parts split cleanly**: every fact has exactly one part to sit under: no two purposes claim one job, and the parts together leave nothing of their lead out. A fact two parts share sits in one, and the other names that part as `{id}`.
- **Parts read alike**: the purposes under one lead share one form, each opening with the verb that says what its part is for, an area's and a state's included, and so do the entries of one list.
- **Order by one principle**: the parts under a lead come in an order a reader can name in a phrase, such as the order of flow, the order of time or from the outside in. Nodes are written in that order.
- **Each text stands alone**: a description is understood without the text before it. It points at nothing outside itself by `it`, `this`, `the above` or `그것`, and ties to its lead by a word of the lead or a sibling named as `{id}`, the known word first and the new fact last.
- **Detail under the narrowest lead**: a detail sits under the lowest lead that covers it: a fact about one node goes into that node's item, never into the level's `about`. No item only repeats its purpose.
- **One idea a sentence**: each sentence of a description holds one idea, in 25 words at most, in the active voice, its subject the thing that acts; a description holds as many sentences as its ideas. So "`{checker}` reads each contract, refuses a broken one and prints each break with its rule so that the writer can fix it" becomes "`{checker}` reads each contract and refuses a broken one. Each break `{checker}` prints names the rule it breaks." A statement holds one condition a test could pass or fail, with no "and/or", "where possible" or "some".
- **Only names are short**: a title and a node's or an area's label hold 6 words at most, since the page draws each as a heading. No other text has a length limit: a description and a step's note run as long as their content needs, and the page wraps each between words. Cut a description only of what the reader does not need, never to fit a size.

### Reader

- **Reader new to the field**: write for the reader, who knows no word of the subject's field. A field word a sentence needs is a term with its `meaning`, or the same sentence says what it is the first time a text uses it; a step the field takes for granted is said. So "`{store}` is idempotent" becomes "`{store}` keeps a change sent twice only once".
- **Abbreviation spelled out**: an abbreviation the reader may not know becomes a term whose `meaning` writes it out, or the text writes it out with the abbreviation in brackets, such as "certificate signing request (CSR)" or "인증서 서명 요청(CSR)". One every reader knows, such as URL, HTML or CPU, stays bare.
- **No meta narration**: a text says what the subject is and does, never something about the text. It gives no reason for its own wording ("named so to avoid confusion with the queue"), no history or change ("formerly", "now", "renamed from", "the new", "이전에는", "이제는"), no note on how to read it ("see below", "note that", "read this as", "참고로"), and no report of what the owner or a requester asked ("as the owner asked", "요청에 따라"). A reason the subject itself has, such as why a lock is held, is content and stays. Nor does it take the contract, the page, a level or itself as its topic: no "This contract describes", "the following steps", "as shown above", "이 문서는" or "아래에서 설명합니다". It states the fact about the subject directly, so "This level shows how a comment reaches the file" becomes "A comment reaches the file through one queue".
- **No filler**: cut each word that carries no fact: an opening that announces ("It is worth noting that", "기본적으로"), a stock transition ("In addition", "Moreover", "또한", "한편"), an emphasis ("very", "really", "simply", "매우", "정말"), and a sentence that only restates its lead. "Moreover, `{server}` simply keeps each comment" becomes "`{server}` keeps each comment".
- **Hedge only the unknown**: state what a source settles as a plain fact, and a fact that holds under a condition with the condition named: "`{server}` refuses a post over 1 MB", never "`{server}` may sometimes refuse a large post". What no source settles is a gap, as **Seen, not supposed** in `SKILL.md` says, never a hedge such as "probably", "usually tends to" or "~일 수도 있습니다" stacked on a fact.

### Words

- **One word per concept**: a concept keeps one word in every text, and a word is never varied for style, since a second word reads as a second thing. A term is written as the term in a Korean description too, such as `code를 누르면 엽니다`, never by a Korean word for it, which belongs in the term's `avoid`.
- **Names stay English**: every title, label, term and edge label is English whatever the language of the descriptions, since the page's own words, its tag names, item labels and controls, are English and one name must read the same beside them.
- **Label names a thing**: a node's label is a noun phrase that opens with a capital and holds no article, such as "Checker", so it reads as a title on the page; an area's names a group or a place the same way, such as "Three jobs" or "Outside the skill". Only the subject's own name, such as a file name, keeps its own case.
- **Edge reads as a sentence**: a static edge's label opens with a verb, so that `<from label> <label> <to label>` is a true, grammatical sentence that ends where the `to` label follows, such as "writes" or "takes its items from". An `access` edge to a file reads "reads", "writes" or "appends a line to".
- **Name, never spell**: a text names a node, an edge or an area as `{id}` and never spells its label, so a new name changes one record. A label is spelt when it follows `the` in any case, or, holding two words or more, when it is written as the label is; words are matched whatever the space between them; a plural or another spelling is the writer's to find. Only a name, a source's own words, an item of the code form and the subject's words between backticks stay as written. The name shows as its label is written, wherever it stands.
- **Note adds, never repeats**: a step's note says what this step alone shows, in as many sentences as that takes. It names neither end of its own edge, copies no purpose, quotes no label between backticks, which a rename would leave stale, and is left out when it has nothing to add.
- **No drawing**: no text says how a thing is drawn: no position, colour, size or markup, since the page's code decides them. The `text-plain` rule refuses the commonest forms; a hex colour of four or eight digits, a unit other than `px`, such as `2rem` or `50%`, an entity such as `&lt;` and words such as "shown in red" are the writer's to keep out. The subject's own colour, length or style goes between backticks or in an item of the code form.
- **Angle words between backticks**: a placeholder, a comparison or the subject's own markup goes between backticks, such as `` `<plugin>` ``, or in an item of the `code` form, which the page draws as written. The checker refuses a table cell or a tree note that is no text.
- **Quote code, never a name**: a command, a path or the subject's own value inside a sentence goes between backticks, which the page draws as inline code, and lines of the subject's own text, such as a config file, go in a triple-backtick fence, which it draws as a block that keeps their breaks and indent. A name, a term or a plain word takes no mark, since a mark says the words are the subject's own.
- **Formulas in TeX**: a quantity the reader must compute is a formula: `$...$` inside a sentence, `$$...$$` as a centered block of its own, written in the subset the `formula` rule names. A price or a shell variable, such as `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}`, is no formula, and a `$` the subject writes otherwise goes between backticks; the contract's title, an area's or a node's label and a term hold none, since the page draws them as words.

### Korean descriptions

- **Settled word first**: a technical, domain or context-bound word that is no term of the contract, a word of business, technique, theory, a profession or a guideline, is chosen in this order: a settled Sino-Korean word, such as 운영 체제, 의존성, 조건부 확률 or 접근 제어; else the transliteration as 표준국어대사전 or 외래어 표기법 spells it, such as 콘텐츠, 메시지, 액세스 토큰 or 핸드셰이크, never 컨텐츠 or 메세지; else the English word as written, such as access token or handshake. So 오퍼레이팅 시스템 is wrong where 운영 체제 is settled. A plain everyday word, such as 열쇠, 문지기 or 악수, names only an everyday concept, never a field's concept made to look simpler: 문지기 for 접근 제어 and 악수 for 핸드셰이크 are wrong, since the reader who looks the concept up finds only the field's word. A transliteration the reader may not know carries its English once, in brackets with no space, such as 프롬프트(prompt). An identifier, a file name, a command and a product name stay as the subject writes them. The word chosen is kept in one spelling and one spacing, and one that must never vary becomes a term.
- **Both particles after a name**: after `{id}` write a particle of one form, such as 의, 에, 에서, 도 or 만, or both forms of one that has two. After a term or an English word, whose sound the writer knows, write the one form that sound takes. A particle joins the word before it with no space: `{door}을(를)`, `code를`, `URL로`.
- **Polite ending**: a sentence closes in `-니다`. An entry or a note that is no sentence is a phrase with no full stop.
- **Korean, not translated**: the actor is the subject of an active verb. Write a plain particle or verb for `~에 의해`, `~를 통해`, `되어지다`, `~를 수행합니다` and `~를 가지고 있습니다`, and drop a chain of `의`, a `들` on each noun, and `그것` or `이것`. A dense Korean sentence splits the same way as an English one: "`{server}`은(는) 의견을 받아 대기열에 넣고 번호를 돌려주며 세션이 읽을 때까지 보관합니다" becomes "`{server}`은(는) 의견을 받아 대기열에 넣습니다. 의견마다 번호를 돌려줍니다. 세션이 읽을 때까지 의견을 보관합니다."
