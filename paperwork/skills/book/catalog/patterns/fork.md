# fork: two confusable concepts separated by one question with two answers
family: visual
elements: graph, alt
differs: one question with two answers separates two confusable concepts, so the drawing is a decision, not a hierarchy. structure cannot force this: its links say contains or depends on

## Why it works
The figure puts the pair side by side and splits them with a single question the reader can answer for any case (fig-fork). It prevents two definitions that read alike.
The question is about the case, not the word: an observable fact that sends a case left or right.

## Parts
- graph: one root question node and two answer nodes, each naming a concept and one example.
- alt: the question and the two answers.

## Examples
Source book: the UX book (ids are its card or figure ids, with the unit in parentheses).
- fig-chat-regen-edit (u-chat): regenerate and edit-and-resend, split by whether the input is kept or changed. Essential content: regenerate keeps the input; edit changes it.
- fig-col-facet-tag (u-collection): facet and tag, split by whether the label comes from a fixed list. Essential content: from a list is a facet; made up as you go is a tag.
- fig-spec-fork (u-speculate): speculative execution and optimistic display, split by whether the user has seen anything when the guess is wrong. Essential content: nothing seen means discard; something seen means undo.
Elsewhere: Weather and climate, split by "over what span of time is the claim made?"
# Compose and vary
- Combines with: sort-bins (a card sorts cases by the fork), explain-why.
- Vary: choose a pair readers actually mix up; test that a stranger can answer the question for a new case.
- Usual failure: the question is the definition of one concept in disguise.

## Seed
Can a reader who has never seen the terms send a new case to the right side?
