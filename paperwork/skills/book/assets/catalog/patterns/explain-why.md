# explain-why: the reader picks the cause behind an observed fact, not the symptom or a label
family: problem
elements: stem, exhibit, answer, rival
differs: the answer is a cause or mechanism the exhibit does not display, chosen against a symptom or a label; the reader needs the idea, not only the picture. read-exhibit cannot force this: its answer is read off the exhibit

## Why it works
The card shows a fact and asks for the mechanism that makes it so. It prevents answers that name the phenomenon (card-claim) and rewards the reader who holds the idea.
Rivals are plausible causes that are real elsewhere but cannot account for this exhibit (card-unique, card-rival).

## Parts
- stem: the observation to explain; no hint of the mechanism.
- exhibit: the case in which the fact can be seen.
- answer / rival: each rival has a `belief` and `right_when`, a condition under which it would be the cause, ruled out by a fact in the exhibit.

## Examples
Source book: the UX book (ids are its card or figure ids, with the unit in parentheses).
- ask-cycle-review (u-cycle): users keep pressing "save" in an app that saves itself. Essential content: the visible button teaches a wrong model (saving is manual), so the fault is on the evaluation side, not in finding the control.
- ask-cmd-auto (u-command): a camera app replaces three controls with one button. Essential content: the complexity moves to the device, which guesses per scene.
- ask-col-count (u-collection): a filter screen puts a count in brackets after each value. Essential content: the count tells where a choice leads before it is made, so empty results are avoided.
Elsewhere: A bakery loaf is dense after a recipe change; which change in the method explains it?
# Compose and vary
- Combines with: near-miss, spot-defect (cause at a marked part).
- Vary: choose an observation that has one cause the unit has taught and two causes from adjacent ideas.
- Usual failure: the right option restates the symptom in other words, or a rival is also true.

## Seed
For each rival, say when it would be the cause; does the exhibit rule that out?
