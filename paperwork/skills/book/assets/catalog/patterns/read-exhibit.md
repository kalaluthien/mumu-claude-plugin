# read-exhibit: the reader reads one exhibit and answers a cause, outcome or move from what is there
family: problem
elements: stem, exhibit, answer, rival
differs: the answer sits in what the exhibit shows and how it reads (an omission, a scale, a salient part); the exhibit may be fully ordinary, no hidden mechanism. explain-why cannot force this: its answer is a cause behind the fact, not a reading of it

## Why it works
A single exhibit carries all the evidence; the reader must read it correctly before applying the idea. It prevents cards whose answer is a rule recalled without looking (card-exhibit).
Wrong options are readings an unprepared person makes of the same exhibit: the most salient part, the first number, the familiar shape.

## Parts
- stem: what to read from the exhibit (cause, outcome, next move), without pointing at material outside it.
- exhibit: carries every fact the options use (facts-shown).
- answer / rival: the right reading with a why that points to the part that supports it; rivals cite a real part with the wrong inference.

## Examples
Source book: the UX book (ids are its card or figure ids, with the unit in parentheses).
- ask-per-order (u-percept): a dot legend of four colours; why do new users keep opening the legend? Essential content: colour has no order.
- ask-present-axis (u-present): three rows of trend lines each scaled to its own range; why do two different rises look equal? Essential content: per-row axes erase the scale.
- ask-per-area (u-percept): three rectangles of different width and height; what does a first-time viewer compare? Essential content: area, not the two sides separately.
Elsewhere: A bar chart of monthly sales cut at 90; the reader says what the cut does to the 2x difference.
# Compose and vary
- Combines with: spot-defect (marks), compare-designs (several exhibits).
- Vary: the kind of exhibit (screen, log, chart, contract); keep the evidence inside it.
- Usual failure: the exhibit shows the answer in text, or the stem points at material not shown (stem-self-contained).

## Seed
Does the answer require something visible only in the exhibit?
