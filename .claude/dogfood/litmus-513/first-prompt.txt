A spec for the same task already exists as a flat 15-question rubric scored yes 2 / ambiguous 1 / no 0: .claude/dogfood/litmus-513/source-rubric.md. Migrate it into tiers of binary criteria, citing it as provenance.

No one can answer questions during this run, so here are the interview answers; take them as the person's and record them as such:
- Deliverable and reader: the hand-back; its reader is the system's owner, who picks one option from it and must be able to trust every number in it without recounting.
- Worst plausible failure: a hand-back whose counts or kept/added/removed lists do not match the model files (stated numbers that are wrong, or a Scenario or rule dropped without saying so). Next worst: a hand-back with no per-option counts, so the owner cannot compare.
- Differences to ignore: wording, section order, table versus list, and which equally good option is recommended. A tier mean that differs by less than 0.1 is a tie.
- Judge: a fresh Claude Sonnet subagent that reads the answer and the model files and may count with a short Python script. No human labels exist; the judge runs three times, so the calibration is estimated.
- Use: eval, comparing two versions of the playbook that produces the hand-backs.
- Exchange pairs: matching the model files beats everything else; any count shown at all for every option beats a single answer stated in prose; whether the recommended option is first or the counts improve is lowest. Settle any other pair yourself and record that you did.
- Mask: an answer whose numbers or lists contradict its model files must not gain from a better lower tier.
